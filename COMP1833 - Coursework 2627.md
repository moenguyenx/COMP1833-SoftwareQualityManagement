COMP1833 – Coursework Specification for 2026/2027 Academic Session

The coursework is to be completed by a group of Four (4) to Five Students (5) based on
the Case Study provided in the Appendix. Submission deadline is 2nd December 2026 at
5 pm (UK time). Your answers are NOT generated by ChatGPT nor similar applications. In
case AI-generated content is included in the coursework, it needs to be enclosed in quotation
marks and properly referenced. Otherwise, if detected, it may be considered plagiarism.

Part 1: Project planning based on the Case Study (see Appendix) - worth 20% (Group
work)

You should write a project description document to describe the project, create a meeting
policy document to organise the meetings for the project description, and discuss potential
issues for the project to enhance project planning.

Deliverables from Part 1:

1.1: Project description document. Please introduce a diagram to support your project
description. (10 marks)

1.2: Meeting policy document for the project description. (5 marks)

1.3:  Discuss  the  legal,  social,  ethical,  and  professional  issues  for  the  project.  (5
marks)

Part 2: Software  Quality Assurance (SQA) in your project  - worth  20%  (Individual
and Peer review)

You  should  individually  list  two  product  functionalities  and  write  a  quality  assurance
document  to  illustrate  the  architectural  blocks  such  as  data  structures  of  the  project. The
architectural blocks should be related to the product functionalities.

Please note that:

•  Each team member must the two functionalities different from everybody else.
•  Each  team  member  must  have  the  architectural  blocks  reviewed  by  other  team

members.

Deliverables from Part 2:

2.1: Architectural blocks. (10 marks) – Individual

2.2: Peer review report. (10 marks) – Peer review

Part 3: Software Quality Plan (SQP) for your project - worth 20% (Group work)

Using the IEEE 730-2014 standard as a guide, you should write a no less than three-page
tailored  document  on  the  following  topics  based  on  your  project:  Documentation  (user
documentation and configuration management) and Management (roles and responsibilities,
and quality assurance estimated resources).

Deliverables from Part 3:

3.1:  Documentation-based  SQP  for  User  documentation  and  Configuration
Management. (10 marks)

3.2: Management-based SQP for Roles and responsibilities, and Quality assurance
estimated resources. (10 marks)

Part 4: Software Requirements Specification (SRS) and Use Cases for your project -
worth 20% (Individual work)

Each  team  member  selects  a  sub-topic  of  the  SRS:  performance,  security,  portability,
usability, and maintainability with the support of the ISO/IEC 25010 standard. You should
individually write a brief document containing the description of two requirements as part
of the SRS in your selected sub-topic. Furthermore, describe two use cases for your product
based on the selected sub-topic.

Deliverables from Part 4:

4.1: SRS. (10 marks)

4.2: Use cases. (10 marks)

Part  5:  Black  Box  Testing  and  Risk  Management  in  your  project  -  worth  20%
(Individual work)

You should individually conduct a black box testing of your listed functionalities (in Part 2
above) and write a brief document containing your testing and its results. Furthermore, you
should individually carry out risk management for the functionalities.

Deliverables from part 5:

5.1: Black box testing for functionalities I and II. (10 marks)

5.2: Please explain two (2) potential product risks from your functionalities or black
box testing results from Section 5.1 above and implement appropriate controls for
the risks. (10 marks)

Appendix: Case Study

The Brief

A Software Technology Company, SATZ, has been hired to transform the daily operations
of healthcare services in Manchester, United Kingdom. The brief is to provide an Artificial
Intelligence  (AI)-powered  Healthcare  Management  software  product  that  enhances
healthcare services in Manchester. Utilising patients’, staff, facilities, and third-party service
providers' information across the healthcare sector, the software product should be able to
provide real-time insights into the daily operations for requesting, accessing, automating,
optimising, and managing healthcare services. Furthermore, the transformation is required
to improve the safety, performance, maintainability, and security of the services provided.
Enhanced data availability and access across the staff, facilities, and service providers are
required  to  ensure  continuous  operations  in  the  event  of  any  service  downtime.  Backup
systems  are  also  required  to  support  continuous  operations.  Reduced  costs  of  healthcare
services are needed to  enhance the affordability of the services for the  patients. The first
contact  your  Software  Quality  Assurance  team  had  with  the  healthcare  regulator  has
identified the following information:

•  The patients and staff use healthcare services that are not integrated with AI and are

unable to have full visibility of daily operations and services available.

•  Most of the facilities are not capable of maintaining themselves.

•  Service  providers  currently  use  a  variety  of  resources,  such  as  sensors  and
controllers,  for  increased  tracking  and  automation  across  the  healthcare  services
network. To request user privacy from the service providers, users normally complete
a web-based/online form.

•  Manchester  Healthcare  Entity  (MHE)  manages  the  healthcare  services  in
Manchester. Although the MHE is keen to develop the new AI-powered healthcare
management software product, it does not currently have the resources to support
this.  They  tried  to  deploy  an  advanced  healthcare  management  software  product
before.  Unfortunately,  the  contracted  firm  overshot  the  budget  and  timeline.  The
project was therefore abandoned. The contractor folded, and MHE was left with an
unusable prototype software product with no documentation.

•  There is very little communication between service providers and the staff. Limited
training is provided to staff. Many patients in the UK struggle to utilise the healthcare
services  provided  due  to  limited  knowledge  of  the  product  and  inadequate
information  available.  Furthermore,  there  is  no  real-time  collaboration  among
service providers.

•  MHE solely relies on its existing software product that manages healthcare services,
thus at risk of a single point of failure if the software product becomes unavailable
at any time.

•  MHE  is  interested  in  a  reward  scheme  and  enhanced  self-service  for  users  to

encourage the use of the new software product.

•  The  patients  and  staff  are  interested  in  continuous  service  improvement,  privacy

options and enhancements, and healthcare services sustainability.

Definitions

This is a level seven Degree coursework and therefore, as well as a demonstrating that you
have learnt some facts or skills, you are being assessed on your ability to research, think,
and reason and then articulate your findings and conclusions.

You will be assessed on the following points:

Knowledge & Comprehension:
•  A clear demonstration of background reading and research into the issues discussed.
•  A demonstration of your understanding of the field, i.e., clearly identifying and

enumerating the fundamental issues, use of correct terminology and facts including
knowledge of the existence and names of methods, classifications, abstractions,
generalisations, and theories.

•  Discussion summarising the topic area and ability to extrapolate beyond the given

situation.

•  Can explain or summarise information giving a good account of work done by others

and reporting ideas intelligibly with accuracy and thoroughness and without introducing
gross distortions.

Analysis, Application & Synthesis
•  Able to apply abstractions and concrete situations, e.g., use of examples to illustrate and

support your argument.

•  General organizational structures can be identified.
•  Assumptions can be recognised.
•  Can produce sensible, reasoned, and substantiated criticism and suggest alternatives.
•  Does not indulge in pointless and unsubstantiated criticism.
•  Able to combine elements or parts in such a way as to produce a pattern or structure

that was not clearly there before.

Evaluation / critique
•  Demonstration of insight.
•  A strong argument supporting or rejecting the technique with a sound conclusion given

your stated premises.

•  Can make qualitative and quantitative judgments about the value of methods, processes,

or artefacts.

LEARNING OUTCOMES

1.  Reflect on the role and management of software quality teams and the principles and
practices of software quality management through modern engineering and software
development methods. Be able to evaluate, select, and utilise appropriate standards,
procedures, tools, techniques, and methodologies to assist the management of given
project  types.  Develop  innovative  approaches  to  enhance  software  quality  and
evaluate associated processes and outcomes.

2.  Critically review software quality literature and current developments, understand
software  processes  and  quality  issues,  and  apply  quality  assurance  principles  and
quality  management  planning  throughout  the  development  life  cycle.  Critically
evaluate current approaches and select those suitable for given application areas and
types of projects.

3.  Understand and apply risk management and peer review process in software quality
management and software development processes, as well as appropriate metrics for
estimation  and  quantitative  management.  Understand  the  role  of  validation  and
verification within the development process.

4.  Critically  appraise  and  understand  the  legal,  social,  ethical,  and  professional
frameworks and issues related to building modern enterprise software systems and
evaluate alternative development lifecycles and strategies.
