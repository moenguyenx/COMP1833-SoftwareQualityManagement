# Referencing Guide: Cite Them Right Harvard vs IEEE Comparison

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Rubric Alignment**: **Dimension D5 (Academic Referencing)** carries 20% of the overall evaluation criteria. To achieve **80–100% Exceptional**, you must demonstrate *"Evidence of determining extensive list of outstanding resources. All sources used are acknowledged in the text and reference list using correct academic citation."*  
> **University Standards**: The University of Greenwich accepts **Cite Them Right Harvard** and **IEEE Referencing Style** for computing and engineering degree programs.

---

## ⚖️ Style Comparison: Harvard vs IEEE

| Feature | Cite Them Right Harvard | IEEE Style |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Citation System** | Author-Date system | Numeric system |
| **In-Text Marker** | `(Sommerville, 2020)` or `(IEEE, 2014, p. 12)` | `[1]` or `[1, pp. 12-14]` |
| **Multiple Authors (≥3)**| `(Bass et al., 2021)` | `Bass et al. [2]` or simply `[2]` |
| **Reference List Order**| Alphabetical by author's surname | Numerical in order of first appearance in text |
| **Best Suited For** | Narrative and evaluative prose | Compact technical specifications & code reports |

## 📖 How to Reference Different Source Types

### 1. International Standards (IEEE & ISO)
The coursework relies heavily on IEEE 730, ISO 25010, ISO 31000, and IEEE 1028.

#### In Cite Them Right Harvard:
- **In-text**: *(IEEE, 2014)* or *(ISO/IEC, 2011)*
- **Reference List**:
  - IEEE (2014) *IEEE Std 730-2014: IEEE Standard for Software Quality Assurance Processes*. Piscataway, NJ: IEEE Computer Society. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1109/IEEESTD.2014.6835311.
  - ISO/IEC (2011) *ISO/IEC 25010:2011 Systems and software engineering — Systems and software Quality Requirements and Evaluation (SQuaRE) — System and software quality models*. Geneva: International Organization for Standardization.

#### In IEEE Style:
- **In-text**: *"...as specified in IEEE Standard 730 [1] and ISO/IEC 25010 [2]..."*
- **Reference List**:
  - `[1]` *IEEE Standard for Software Quality Assurance Processes*, IEEE Std 730-2014, 2014, doi: 10.1109/IEEESTD.2014.6835311.
  - `[2]` *Systems and software engineering — Systems and software Quality Requirements and Evaluation (SQuaRE) — System and software quality models*, ISO/IEC 25010:2011, 2011.

---

### 2. Primary Textbooks & Monographs

#### In Cite Them Right Harvard:
- **In-text**: *(Pressman and Maxim, 2020)* or *Sommerville (2020, p. 88)*
- **Reference List**:
  - Pressman, R.S. and Maxim, B.R. (2020) *Software Engineering: A Practitioner's Approach*. 9th edn. New York: McGraw-Hill Education.
  - Sommerville, I. (2020) *Software Engineering*. 10th edn. Harlow: Pearson Education.
  - Bass, L., Clements, P. and Kazman, R. (2021) *Software Architecture in Practice*. 4th edn. Boston: Addison-Wesley.

#### In IEEE Style:
- **In-text**: *"...according to Pressman and Maxim [3]..."*
- **Reference List**:
  - `[3]` R. S. Pressman and B. R. Maxim, *Software Engineering: A Practitioner's Approach*, 9th ed. New York: McGraw-Hill Education, 2020.
  - `[4]` I. Sommerville, *Software Engineering*, 10th ed. Harlow, UK: Pearson Education, 2020.

---

### 3. UK Statutory Legislation & Regulatory Frameworks

#### In Cite Them Right Harvard:
- **In-text**: *(Data Protection Act 2018)* or *(National Data Guardian, 2020)*
- **Reference List**:
  - Great Britain (2018) *Data Protection Act 2018: Chapter 12*. London: The Stationery Office. Available at: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/contents (Accessed: 24 October 2026).
  - National Data Guardian (2020) *The Caldicott Principles: Eight Principles to Ensure People’s Information is Handled Safely and Sensitively*. London: Department of Health and Social Care.
  - British Computer Society (2022) *BCS Code of Conduct for Information Technology Professionals*. Swindon: British Computer Society.

#### In IEEE Style:
- **In-text**: *"...under the UK Data Protection Act 2018 [5] and the Caldicott Principles [6]..."*
- **Reference List**:
  - `[5]` *Data Protection Act 2018*, c. 12, UK Parliament, 2018. [Online]. Available: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/contents
  - `[6]` National Data Guardian, *The Caldicott Principles*, UK Dept. of Health and Social Care, London, 2020.
  - `[7]` British Computer Society, *BCS Code of Conduct for Information Technology Professionals*, Swindon, UK, 2022.

---

### 4. Peer-Reviewed Academic Journal Articles

#### In Cite Them Right Harvard:
- **In-text**: *(Topol, 2019)* or *(O’Regan, 2018)*
- **Reference List**:
  - Topol, E.J. (2019) ‘High-performance medicine: the convergence of human and artificial intelligence’, *Nature Medicine*, 25(1), pp. 44–56. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-018-0300-7.
  - O’Regan, G. (2018) ‘Software Quality and Software Quality Assurance’, in *Concise Guide to Software Engineering*. Cham: Springer, pp. 241–268.

#### In IEEE Style:
- **In-text**: *"...as established by Topol [8]..."*
- **Reference List**:
  - `[8]` E. J. Topol, "High-performance medicine: the convergence of human and artificial intelligence," *Nature Medicine*, vol. 25, no. 1, pp. 44–56, 2019, doi: 10.1038/s41591-018-0300-7.

## ⚡ Checkpoint: The 5 Most Common Referencing Penalties

1. **Missing In-Text Linkage**: Having sources in your bibliography that are never cited in the body of the report.
2. **Missing Page Numbers**: Quoting direct phrases without indicating page numbers (`Sommerville, 2020, p. 114`).
3. **Incomplete URL Metadata**: Writing just `www.google.com` or `https://github.com` without article title, author, date, and `Accessed: [Date]`.
4. **Style Inconsistency**: Mixing Harvard author-dates with IEEE bracket numbers in the same document. **Pick one style for your group and maintain it throughout**.
5. **Over-reliance on Non-Academic Blogs**: Citing random Medium or Wikipedia articles instead of IEEE/ISO standards and peer-reviewed software engineering textbooks.
