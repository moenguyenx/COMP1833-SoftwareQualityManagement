## 🎯 Learning Outcomes (LOs) Mapped to Coursework Parts

The coursework is explicitly designed to assess four core Master's-level Learning Outcomes:

| Learning Outcome | Focus Area | Addressed in Coursework |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LO1** | **Software Quality Teams & Management**: Reflect on roles, management of software quality teams, principles of SQA through modern engineering/development methods; evaluate and select standards (IEEE 730-2014, ISO 25010), tools, and methodologies. | **Part 1** (Planning, Meeting Governance) & **Part 3** (Software Quality Plan, RACI, QA resources) |
| **LO2** | **Critical Review & SQA Lifecycle Planning**: Critically review software quality literature, understand software processes and quality issues, and apply quality assurance principles throughout the development lifecycle. | **Part 1.1** (Architecture & System Scope), **Part 3** (Tailored SQP), & **Part 4** (ISO 25010 SRS) |
| **LO3** | **Risk Management, Peer Review & Verification/Validation (V&V)**: Understand and apply risk management and peer review processes; utilize metrics for quantitative management; evaluate V&V roles. | **Part 2.2** (Peer Review Report - IEEE 1028), **Part 5.1** (Black Box Testing - V&V), and **Part 5.2** (Risk Management - ISO 31000) |
| **LO4** | **Legal, Social, Ethical & Professional (LSEP) Frameworks**: Critically appraise and understand LSEP frameworks related to enterprise software systems; evaluate alternative development lifecycles and strategies. | **Part 1.3** (LSEP Analysis: UK GDPR, Caldicott Principles, BCS Code of Conduct) |

## 🔍 Key University Directives & Level 7 Assessment Criteria

According to the specification (Page 4), Level 7 Degree coursework evaluates your capacity to **research, think, reason, synthesize, and evaluate**:

1. **Knowledge & Comprehension**:
   - Clear demonstration of background reading and research beyond the brief.
   - Rigorous use of correct software engineering terminology, standards, abstractions, and theories.
   - Accurate summarization of prior literature without distortions.

2. **Analysis, Application & Synthesis**:
   - Application of concrete examples to illustrate abstract quality concepts.
   - Explicit identification of architectural and organizational structures.
   - Reasoned, substantiated criticism with justified alternative solutions.
   - Combining separate parts to form a coherent, robust software architecture.

3. **Evaluation & Critique**:
   - Demonstration of original critical insight.
   - Well-defended arguments supporting or rejecting specific technical choices (e.g., microservices vs monolithic, cloud vs on-premise, Gitflow vs Trunk-based development).
   - Quantitative and qualitative judgments regarding SQA methods, tools, and artifacts.
