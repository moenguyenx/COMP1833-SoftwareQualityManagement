## 📝 Master Academic Template (Ready to Adapt)

### 1.0 Project Background & Operational Context
The healthcare delivery landscape in the Greater Manchester metropolitan region operates under acute clinical demands, constrained municipal resources, and strict regulatory oversight. Manchester Healthcare Entity (MHE) manages healthcare services across this region. However, current operations are constrained by services that are not integrated with AI, offer only partial visibility of daily operations, and rely on a single existing software product.

To overcome these limitations, SATZ, a software technology company, has been commissioned to design and deliver the **SATZ Manchester HealthAI Platform**. This platform is an intelligent, event-driven management system that brings together data from patients, staff, facilities, and third-party service providers to support real-time requesting, accessing, automating, optimising, and managing of healthcare services.

### 2.0 Problem Statement & Critical Vulnerabilities
An assessment of MHE's operational baseline, based on the initial regulator contact, identifies the following deficiencies:
1. **Limited Operational Visibility**: Patients and staff use services that are not integrated with AI and cannot see the full picture of daily operations and available services.
2. **Infrastructure Fragility**: Most facilities cannot maintain themselves, which risks unforecast downtime of critical equipment (e.g., sterilisation units, diagnostic scanners).
3. **Poor Communication and Collaboration**: There is very little communication between service providers and staff, and no real-time collaboration among service providers.
4. **Training and Patient Knowledge Gaps**: Staff receive limited training, and many patients struggle to use services because of limited knowledge of the product and inadequate information.
5. **Single Point of Failure (SPOF)**: MHE relies solely on its existing software product, so any outage threatens continuity of services.
6. **Prior Project Failure**: A previous attempt to deploy a similar product overshot its budget and timeline, was abandoned, and left an undocumented prototype after the contractor folded. This project therefore applies a formal software quality plan (IEEE, 2014) and rigorous configuration management.

### 3.0 Strategic Project Objectives & Scope
The SATZ HealthAI initiative commits to six objectives:
- **Operational Transparency & AI Optimisation**: Use AI to route patients to the right department, forecast demand, and predict equipment degradation, with a human always available when the AI is unsure.
- **Resilience & High Availability**: Run a primary cloud site with an automatically failing-over hot-standby site and regular backups, targeting a Recovery Point Objective (RPO) < 1 minute and Recovery Time Objective (RTO) < 5 minutes.
- **Enhanced Engagement & Usability**: Provide an accessible patient self-service portal with a wellness reward scheme, plus training materials for staff, to encourage adoption.
- **Privacy & Compliance by Design**: Replace manual privacy web forms with in-app consent management compliant with the UK Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR.
- **Affordability & Sustainability**: Reduce service costs through automation, preventive maintenance, and better use of resources.
- **Continuous Service Improvement**: Collect patient and staff feedback to drive ongoing improvement.

**Scope & Phasing**: To avoid the "big bang" failure of the earlier project, delivery is phased (Sommerville, 2020). Phase 1 is a pilot at a 100-bed community hospital in Greater Manchester, followed by wider roll-out once quality targets are met.

### 5.0 High-Level System Architecture

The platform uses a layered, microservices-based, event-driven design chosen for fault isolation and scalability (Bass, Clements and Kazman, 2021):
- **Users & Channels**: web and mobile interfaces built to WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility for patients, staff, facility teams, and providers.
- **Access & Security**: API gateway with firewall, role-based login, in-app consent management, and encryption in transit and at rest.
- **Application Services**: eight independent functions (see 7.0), so a fault in one cannot stop another.
- **AI & Integration**: an event bus, AI models, an IoT gateway for sensor data, and standard HL7 FHIR links to NHS systems and ambulance dispatch.
- **Data Storage**: relational database for records, time-series database for sensor data, and an append-only audit log.
- **Infrastructure & Resilience**: primary cloud site, hot-standby site, and immutable backups.

### 6.0 Operational Process & Information Flow

> *Insert [[07 - Diagrams & Visual Models/Diagram 2 - Software & Operational Flow|Figure 3: End-to-End Operational and Telemetry Software Flow]] here.*

Two main flows operate across the platform:
1. **Patient care flow**: A patient books an appointment and types their symptoms. The Privacy Broker checks consent before processing. The AI triage function recommends a department; it does **not** diagnose. Red-flag symptoms trigger emergency advice (999/ED) and a nurse alert. Confident results offer a slot directly, while vague or low-confidence cases are passed to a triage nurse. Staff are notified, third-party transport is coordinated through real-time messaging where needed, and reward points are credited on check-in.
2. **Predictive maintenance flow**: Facility sensors stream readings continuously. Fixed safety rules and a predictive model detect abnormal behaviour. The platform raises a graded alert and opens a maintenance work order for the estates team or contractor before equipment fails. Technician feedback is used to improve the model. The platform alerts and recommends; it does not automatically shut down clinical-critical equipment.

### 7.0 Core Functional Capabilities
- **Patient Self-Service & Privacy**: booking, service information, and consent control.
- **Wellness Reward Scheme**: points for check-ins, screenings, and surveys.
- **Real-Time AI Patient Triage**: symptom-to-department routing with nurse escalation.
- **IoT Predictive Maintenance & Alerting**: early fault detection and automatic work orders.
- **Clinical Collaboration**: shared real-time messaging between staff and providers.
- **Provider Dispatch & Inventory**: third-party dispatch and stock tracking.
- **Service Feedback Loop**: patient and staff feedback feeding improvement.
- **Audit Trail & Compliance**: tamper-proof record of system actions for regulators.

### 8.0 Eliminating Single Points of Failure (SPOF)
To maintain continuous operations as the case study requires:
- **Stateless Microservices**: application instances run across multiple availability zones; if one fails, it is restarted automatically.
- **Database Geo-Redundancy**: data is replicated from the primary cloud region to a hot-standby region in the UK (e.g., UK South and UK West). If the primary fails, automated failover redirects traffic without manual intervention, targeting RTO < 5 minutes and RPO < 1 minute.
- **Backups**: regular, immutable backups allow restoration after data loss or corruption.
- **Monitoring**: continuous health checks and alerts detect failures early.
